1. MAD-LEO: A Maneuver-Annotated Orbital Dataset for LEO Satellites with Tiered
Multi-Source Evidence
Zhixin Guo, Qi Shi, Xiaofan Xu, Linqiang Ge, Hua Zhu, Liyan Ben, Bendian Nie, Yuanrui Zhao, Xiaohan
Li
8 September 2026 — arXiv preprint + public dataset
Direct paper — arXiv
This is the most important new item this week for your research.
The core contribution is not a detector but a real maneuver benchmark with independently sourced
evidence. The mission-reported subset contains 1,134 maneuver events from 11 LEO geodetic/altimetry
satellites, spanning 1992–2026. Labels come from mission-published maneuver histories rather than
being generated by an anomaly detector. Each event is then cross-referenced with TLEs, precise-orbit
products, and SLR observations.
arXiv
The included spacecraft are TOPEX/Poseidon, Jason-1/2/3, CryoSat-2, HY-2A, SARAL/AltiKa, Sentinel-
3A/3B, Sentinel-6A and SWOT. In addition, an operational subset contains operator ephemerides and
TLEs for 6,785 Starlink spacecraft over 107 continuous hours. The Starlink subset is intentionally left
unlabeled because no independently validated maneuver truth is available.
arXiv
Dataset design
Each reported maneuver is surrounded by a 30 h evaluation window:
[t − 6h, t + 24h].
m m
The asymmetry is deliberate because a maneuver may enter the public catalog only after some delay.
Event windows are assigned an evidence tier:
A : TLE + precise orbit + SLR
B : TLE + precise orbit
C : incomplete auxiliary evidence.
There are 754 tier-A, 194 tier-B and 186 tier-C maneuver events. Importantly, these tiers characterize
supporting evidence availability; they do not redefine the mission-reported label.
arXiv
The release also contains 1,139 no-event windows, plus a 274-window subset matched for evidence
coverage. Fourteen nominal windows are explicitly flagged as possible unreported maneuvers because
their TLE-derived semi-major-axis response exceeds 20 m.
arXiv
That last point is particularly important: the authors acknowledge that nominal labels are not necessarily
perfect.
Realism and reproducibility
The release is unusually strong:
40 files, about 4.3 GB;

exact mission-report source lines;
TLE histories;
parsed precise-orbit evidence;
approximately 2.1 million SLR normal points and 24 million full-rate observations;
43.36 million Starlink ephemeris-state rows;
machine-readable provenance and quality flags.
arXiv
The dataset is publicly released under CC BY 4.0. The authors also provide seven technical-validation
experiment suites.
arXiv
Detection latency
No ML detector is benchmarked as the main contribution, so there is no reported MTTD or event-delay
curve. However, the dataset explicitly contains event epochs, operation intervals and a
field, which makes a proper latency evaluation possible.

The detector is retrained monthly:

Uncertainty/calibration
Thresholding is bootstrap-based, with phase-specific empirical envelopes. This is not calibrated

Main limitation
The method requires a known and reasonably stable cycle period T.
Orbital data are more complicated because periodicity may exist at several frequencies:
orbital period;

sampling cadence;

A single periodic envelope could therefore be too simplistic.
Why it matters
This raises an experiment that is highly relevant for your residuals:
1. compute nominal residuals as a function of orbital phase;
2. test whether residual variance/quantiles depend systematically on phase;
3. compare global thresholding with phase-conditioned calibration.
Formally:

where ν is true/mean anomaly or another phase variable.
If false alarms concentrate at specific orbital phases, a phase-dependent detector may improve
performance more than changing the forecasting architecture.
