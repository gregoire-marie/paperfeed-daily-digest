SSA / satellite-maneuver ML briefing — 14–20 September 2026
This week produced no strong new paper directly on orbital-maneuver detection comparable to MAD-
LEO last week. The worthwhile material is instead methodological. Two papers are particularly relevant to
your current residual-based approach: GSLAD, because it asks whether anomalies are more visible as
changes in inter-variable structure than as forecast residuals, and Jensen et al., because it addresses
adaptive thresholding under distribution shift without immediately contaminating the nominal reference.

4. SOTER: A Generative Time-Series Foundation Model for Wearable Human
Physiological Signals
Fangke Chen, Sirry Chen, Wei Chen, Zhongyu Wei
15 September 2026 — arXiv preprint.
Direct paper — arXiv
Public implementation
SOTER is not an anomaly detector, but it addresses three properties that general-purpose models such as
TimesFM handle poorly for your problem:
multivariate coupling + irregular timestamps + continuous-time dynamics.
Architecture
The model combines:
1. continuous-time rotary positional encoding;
2. five lower channel-independent temporal layers;
3. one high-level cross-channel layer;
4. power-spectral-density-guided mixture-of-experts;
5. a Neural Controlled Differential Equation / Neural Ordinary Differential Equation decoder.
The timestamp itself, rather than the sequence index, controls positional encoding. The decoder can
therefore be queried at arbitrary future times.
arXiv
This is highly relevant to radar/laser SSA data where

SOTER is pre-trained on 226 billion physiological time points from five public datasets, then evaluated

zero-shot forecasting;
frozen-representation classification;
continuous-time imputation.
It obtains the lowest RMSE on 4/6 zero-shot forecasting datasets, lowest MAE on 5/6, and lowest

5. QUALS: Corpus Equilibrium for Universal Forecasting via Pattern Quantization
and Learnability Synchronization
Yujie Li, Zezhi Shao, Chengqing Yu, Yisong Fu, Weijie Zhu, Yifan Du, Jilin Hu, Bin Yang, Yongjun Xu,
Fei Wang
17 September 2026 — arXiv preprint.
arXiv
Direct paper — arXiv
This is relevant because it shifts attention away from architecture and toward the distribution of
temporal patterns seen during pre-training.
Core idea
Large time-series corpora are heavily imbalanced. Common easy motifs dominate optimization while
uncommon dynamics receive relatively little effective training.
QUALS first quantizes temporal patterns using vector quantization and uniform binning, then modifies
sampling weights according to their observed learnability.
The objective is essentially to prevent:
common/easy patterns ≫ rare/difficult patterns
from determining the representation. The authors report that existing forecasting architectures trained
with QUALS can achieve better zero-shot performance using only a fraction of the original pretraining
data.
arXiv +1
Evaluation
The experiments concern zero-shot forecasting, not anomaly detection. Therefore:
no FAR;
no anomaly calibration;
no detection delay;
no maneuver evaluation.
The evidence should not be overinterpreted.

Your training population may contain very unequal amounts of:

Uniform sampling over observations means the dominant orbital regime receives most gradient updates.
