SSA/ML research brief — 28 September to 4 October 2026
No qualifying publication this week evaluated directly on satellite maneuver labels, orbital-element
residuals, conjunction data, or maneuver-detection latency. The five strongest items are therefore
transferable time-series anomaly-detection contributions. Two are peer-reviewed conference papers;
three are new preprints.
The weekʼs clearest message is methodological: continuously updating a detector is not automatically
better, probabilistic scores are not necessarily calibrated probabilities, and threshold-independent
accuracy can conceal operationally unacceptable detection delay.
1. In a Streaming World, Should You Stand Still? A Comprehensive Benchmark of Anomaly
Detection in Streams
Authors: Magali Parrino, Antoine Ajenjo, Emmanuel Remy, Pierre Stephan, Pierre Senellart, Paul Boniol
Published: 30 September 2026
Source type: Peer-reviewed KDD 2026 paper; new arXiv release
Direct sources:Paper · Code
Technical contribution. StrAD compares three operational regimes that are often conflated:
Static: fit and score with global/offline access.
Online: fit on an initial batch, then score causally without updating model parameters.
Streaming: score sequentially while incrementally updating the model or reference state.
It benchmarks 19 static/online TSAD methods and ten streaming detectors on 17 real datasets, including
SMAP and MSL spacecraft telemetry. TSB-drift extracts real series with identifiable concept drift rather
than relying entirely on synthetic drift.
arxiv.org
Methods and data. The detector pool spans proximity, forecasting, reconstruction, Fourier, tree, hashing,
clustering, and window-updating approaches. Evaluation begins with an initial training batch—potentially
contaminated by anomalies—then proceeds sequentially. Drift types include change points, periodic drift
and random-walk-like changes.
Evaluation and evidence. AUC-PR is the principal metric; the authors deliberately reject delay-tolerant
VUS for this experiment because post-event tolerance conflicts with the streaming objective. Critical-
difference tests and training-size ablations are included. Online, non-updating methods average 64%
higher AUC-PR than streaming methods with the full initial batch, 49% higher at 75%, and 47% higher at
half the batch. CNN is significantly ahead of the best streaming method, SWKNN, on the full benchmark,
although the difference disappears on point-anomaly-only data.
arxiv.org +1
On drifted series, streaming models generally degrade less: 70% of them are more drift-resilient than 70%
of online models. That is relative robustness, however—not evidence that their absolute accuracy
becomes superior. Streaming updates also produce roughly an order of magnitude more inference-time
variability. The accuracy/throughput Pareto frontier includes streaming methods, but the strongest online
CNN still averages approximately 770 scored points/s.
arxiv.org +1
Latency and uncertainty. Inference throughput and runtime variability are reported, but event-level time-
to-detection is not. No predictive uncertainty, score calibration, confidence intervals for alarm rates, or
controlled false-alarm operating points are evaluated.
Implementation relevance. This is the most immediately actionable paper for a maneuver detector. A
satellite pipeline typically has moderate sampling rates and collective anomalies, so it may benefit more
from a capable frozen detector plus separately gated recalibration than from unconditional per-sample
updating.
Limitations and risks.
Drift identification appears retrospective; it does not test a prospective drift alarm.
Generic benchmark drift is not the same as secular orbital evolution, force-model change, sensor
handover, or covariance change.
AUC-PR still averages across thresholds and does not answer “false alerts per object-day.”
Streaming methods are largely lightweight outlier algorithms, making the comparison partly a
capacity comparison.
No direct maneuver-detection delay analysis.
Why it matters. It directly challenges the assumption that online adaptation should be enabled by default.
For orbital residuals, adaptation can learn away a low-thrust maneuver or absorb a sequence of correlated
burns.
2. GT-PSSM: Unified Probabilistic Framework for Stochastic Dynamics Modeling and
Dependency Learning in Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection
Authors: Wonmo Koo, Jaeyeong Lee, Taeseong Yoon, Heeyoung Kim
Published: 28 September 2026
Source type: Preprint
Direct source:Paper
Technical contribution. GT-PSSM combines a probabilistic latent state-space model with a graph
transformer. The PSSM separates latent dynamics from measurement noise; the graph component
models long-range temporal structure and cross-variable dependencies that recurrent PSSMs may miss.
The anomaly score is an expected negative log-likelihood,
s t = ∑ Eq(z t ) [−logp(x i,t ∣ z t ,h t−1 )],
i
with each variableʼs contribution robustly normalized using validation-set median and IQR. Unlike some
baseline implementations, these normalization statistics are not computed from the full test series,
avoiding a genuine online leakage route.
arxiv.org +1
Data. Four standard multivariate industrial datasets:
Dataset Variables Train points Test points Test anomaly rate
WADI 112 118,800 17,280 6%
PSM 25 132,481 87,841 28%
SMD 38 28,479 28,479 9%
SWaT 49 47,520 44,991 12%
Evaluation and evidence. Results are averaged over ten runs and include ordinary F1 and the inflation-
prone point-adjusted F1. Using ordinary F1, GT-PSSM improves the best listed baseline from 0.6749 to
0.7343 on WADI, 0.6335 to 0.6631 on PSM, and 0.6807 to 0.7375 on SMD; it is second on SWaT, 0.8466
versus 0.8530. On all 28 SMD machines, the improvement is much smaller: 0.5272 versus 0.5233 for
NPSR.
arxiv.org +1
Latency and uncertainty. Batch forward-pass times are 0.076 s on WADI and 0.049 s on SMD, but event-
level detection delay is absent. The score is uncertainty-aware because emission variance affects NLL. It
is not demonstrated to be probabilistically calibrated: there are no coverage tests, probability integral
transform diagnostics, reliability curves, or prediction-interval checks.
Serious evaluation caveat. The reported F1 threshold is selected by grid search to maximize each metric.
Unless this search is confined to a validation set—and the wording does not establish that—it acts as an
oracle threshold and can substantially inflate deployable F1.
arxiv.org
Implementation details. The model is materially heavier than the baselines: on WADI it uses 1,832 MiB
and takes 52.6 s per training epoch; on SMD, 591 MiB and 66.9 s. Baseline implementation links are
supplied, but I did not find a public GT-PSSM repository in the paper.
Limitations.
Standard cyber-physical benchmarks have repetitive operating modes and known annotation
problems.
Anomaly prevalence is much higher than in operational maneuver monitoring.
No calibration, false-alert budget, causal delay metric, or missing-observation experiment.
Reconstruction conditions on the current observation; it is not equivalent to forecasting a maneuver
before or immediately at onset.
Why it matters. The probabilistic decomposition is a good match for maneuver research: use a dynamics-
conditioned predictive distribution and score residuals relative to expected heteroscedastic uncertainty,
instead of thresholding raw propagation error.
3. Detect, Explain, Interpret: An End-to-End Benchmark for Time Series Anomaly Detection,
Explainability and Interpretability
Authors: Roberto Stanzione, Jules Barbe, Magali Parrino, Jérémie Fourmann, Paul Boniol
Published: 1 October 2026
Source type: Preprint and public benchmark
Direct sources:Paper · SHAD repository
Technical contribution. SHAD separates three tasks:
1. detecting whether an anomaly exists;
2. explaining which dimensions/components caused the score;
3. converting that evidence into a semantically grounded human interpretation.
The dataset contains 215 approximately 18-hour series, sampled once per minute over 171 variables.
Failures were intentionally executed on a real Scality distributed-storage installation, with expert-recorded
onset, duration, affected component, type and severity. Labels are supplied both globally and per affected
dimension.
arxiv.org +1
Methods and data. Eighteen semi-supervised and unsupervised detectors are compared. Eighty-three
nominal runs provide training data for semi-supervised models. Anomalies cover disk failures, storage-
node failures and degradations, including simultaneous, asynchronous and cascading variants.
For explanation, native per-dimension score contributions are ranked against the component labels. For
interpretation, five frozen LLMs receive statistical features, images or image descriptions and answer a
structured questionnaire about presence, affected dimensions, timing, type and criticality.
Evaluation and evidence.
Detection: VUS-PR with a 25-sample temporal buffer.
Dimension attribution: NDCG.
LLM classification: F1.
LLM temporal/component localization: accuracy.
Nondeterministic detectors are run five times.
Critical-difference testing finds OA, USAD, AE, CNN and unsupervised KMAD statistically
indistinguishable at α = 0.05. arxiv.org +1
The important negative result is that dimension-attribution NDCG remains close to random and frozen
LLMs do not reliably interpret the failures. Supplying oracle anomaly-presence or component information
improves some tasks only modestly; for example, oracle presence improves dimension localization by
0.0719 and oracle components improve temporal localization by 0.1446.
arxiv.org +2
Latency and uncertainty. No detection-delay metric is reported. At one-minute sampling, the 25-point
VUS tolerance represents up to roughly 25 minutes of boundary tolerance, so good VUS-PR need not
mean prompt detection. There is no uncertainty calibration for detector scores or LLM answers.
Implementation relevance. The semantic dataset design is more valuable than the winning detector. An
orbital counterpart should provide:
event-level maneuver intervals;
affected orbital elements or residual channels;
spacecraft/ground-sensor context;
maneuver class, direction and estimated magnitude;
confidence and ambiguity labels.
Limitations. Each series contains only one failure family, all data come from one platform, and failures are
controlled injections rather than naturally occurring incidents. The authors acknowledge both restrictions.
The 25-point tolerance and absence of explicit false-alert accounting limit operational
arxiv.org
conclusions.
Why it matters. It provides a concrete recipe for moving beyond a binary maneuver flag toward root-
cause localization and operator-facing explanations—and strong evidence that raw detector attributions
or zero-shot LLM narration are not yet trustworthy.
4. Have an LLM Write Your Anomaly Detector: Autonomous Discovery of Compact,
Interpretable Detectors for Time Series
Author: David Berghaus
Published: 1 October 2026
Source type: Preprint with executable code
Direct sources:Paper · Anonymous code repository
Technical contribution. An LLM repeatedly edits and evaluates a small NumPy detector rather than
serving as the inference model. The best programs summarize sliding windows with local spectral
features, then score their deviation from normal training windows with a shrinkage-regularized
Mahalanobis distance.
The univariate method uses multi-scale spectral-organization descriptors, robust median/MAD scaling,
95%-weighted shrinkage toward identity, training-tail ranks and a tail-amplifying gate. The multivariate
version stacks six frequency-band powers per channel for an 80-sample window and uses a ridge-
regularized joint covariance.
arxiv.org +1
Search and leakage controls. Candidate programs are selected using synthetic anomalies injected only
into held-out portions of training regions. Real anomaly labels are not used in the search. The injection
family covers freezes, misplaced shapes, period/phase changes, magnitude events and cross-channel
perturbations.
arxiv.org
Evaluation and evidence. The TSB-AD evaluation covers eleven univariate and five multivariate families
using affiliation F-measure, F1-T, standard F1 and VUS-PR. In a focused comparison, the discovered
univariate detector reports VUS-PR 53.9 versus at most 35.0; replacing its descriptor with catch22 drops
it to 28.4. On the broader TAB tables, however, its mean VUS-PR is 0.319 univariate and 0.499 multivariate
—still strong, but less spectacular than the focused percentage-scale table suggests.
arxiv.org +1
Latency and uncertainty. Runtime is tens of milliseconds for typical series and at most a few seconds for
the longest. Complexity is approximately linear in series length. But the paper does not establish causal
windowing or event-level delay, so these are compute times, not actionable detection latencies.
Mahalanobis distance and empirical tail ranks encode relative novelty, not calibrated uncertainty.
Implementation relevance. It is CPU-only, GPU-free and auditable, with complete short implementations.
That makes it an excellent baseline for orbital residual streams, especially for periodicity changes, low-
thrust signatures and phase inconsistencies.
Limitations and leakage risks.
The strongest LLM search was run once per regime, so search variance is under-characterized.
Repeated optimization against a fixed synthetic generator can overfit to its anomaly taxonomy even
without real labels.
The author and model may have prior familiarity with public benchmark conventions.
No leave-one-dataset-family-out program search is shown.
Multivariate covariance may become unstable when channels greatly outnumber normal windows.
No controlled false-alarm or calibration experiment.
Why it matters. The detector itself—not the “LLM scientist” framing—is useful. It supplies a compact
spectral/covariance baseline that could be much harder for complex neural models to beat than raw z-
scores or isolation forests.
5. From Benchmarks to Production: Transferring Time Series Anomaly Detection Methods
for Electricity Production Monitoring
Authors: Nicolas Vautier, Paul Caron, Nardi Xhepi, Félicie Bizeul, Manel Boumghar, Christophe Degouy,
Paul Boniol
Published: 30 September 2026
Source type: Peer-reviewed IEEE ICDE 2026 paper; industrial deployment report
Direct sources:Paper · Code
Technical contribution. TAMIS detects anomalous daily electricity-production forecasts at EDF. Each
example is a 24-hour profile of 48 half-hour values, scored against three years of history. Twenty
interpretable features feed two inexpensive detectors—Gaussian KDE and ASHES, a rank/extremity score
—and XGBoost combines their scores.
Ten features are expert-designed; ten are selected from TSFresh/catch22 candidates using synthetic
anomalies, statistical relevance tests and correlation-based redundancy removal. Previous-day features
are cached. Results are ranked into a short daily expert newsletter, explicitly trading exhaustive recall for
manageable alert volume.
arxiv.org +1
Data and realism. The released THERM, HYDRAU and JO datasets comprise 6,931 expert-annotated
daily windows, including 376 anomalies and thousands of heterogeneous sensors. This is substantially
closer to an operational human-review workflow than most TSAD benchmarks.
Evaluation and evidence. Metrics include AUC-PR, throughput, OOD transfer and VUS-PR on TSB-UAD.
On the three EDF sets, TAMIS variants reach AUC-PR 0.837–0.856 on JO, 0.585–0.691 on HYDRAU and
0.486–0.555 on THERM; raw-series detectors are much worse. The selected two-detector configuration
is reported as 2.4× faster than the largest ensemble. On generic TSB-UAD, TAMIS reaches approximately
0.38 VUS-PR versus about 0.35 for learned model selection.
arxiv.org +1
For transfer, a model trained on JO is tested unchanged on HYDRAU and THERM. All methods degrade,
but TAMIS retains > 0.5 AUC-PR on HYDRAU and loses about 34% on THERM, versus 53% for ASHES.
This is stronger OOD evidence than a random in-domain split, though there are only two target domains.
arxiv.org
Latency and uncertainty. The operational cadence is daily: the full 24-hour forecast is scored and
reported once per day. “Near-real-time” therefore describes post-window computation, not early event
detection. Scores and rankings are not calibrated probabilities, and no uncertainty intervals are provided.
Limitations.
Supervised ensemble weights require labels; cold-start performance relies on corporate transfer.
Synthetic anomaly selection can privilege point, shift and amplitude patterns.
OOD evaluation covers only three related corporate domains.
No prospective operator study reports precision among reviewed alerts, action rate or alert-fatigue
reduction.
Daily-window detection is not directly applicable to rapid maneuver onset.
Why it matters. TAMIS offers the best systems lesson this week: optimize the detector, ranking policy,
cached computation and analyst interface together. For SSA, “top k events per shift” and precision-at-
review-budget may matter more than an unconstrained benchmark F1.
Ranked reading priority
1. Streaming World / StrAD — strongest consequence for adaptation policy and causal evaluation.
2. GT-PSSM — closest methodological fit to uncertainty-aware dynamical residual scoring.
3. SHAD — best blueprint for event, channel and semantic annotations.
4. LLM-written spectral detector — highest-value lightweight baseline; claims need independent
validation.
5. TAMIS — strongest production and human-in-the-loop lessons, but its daily cadence is less
transferable.
Concrete experiments for satellite maneuver detection
1. Frozen versus gated adaptation. Compare a frozen detector, continuous updating, and updating
only after a drift test plus anomaly veto. Report event recall, median/95th-percentile delay, false alerts
per satellite-day and the fraction of low-thrust events absorbed by adaptation.
2. Uncertainty-aware orbital residual score. Replace raw residual norm with predictive NLL using
propagated covariance plus learned heteroscedastic variance. Evaluate calibration separately from
ranking using coverage, PIT histograms, expected calibration error and false-alarm control at fixed
nominal tail probabilities.
3. Spectral-Gaussian baseline. Apply the compact multi-scale spectral descriptor to radial–in-track–
cross-track residuals, equinoctial-element differences and innovation sequences. Use leave-one-
spacecraft and leave-one-orbit-regime-out validation; fit all scaling and covariance parameters
strictly on past nominal data.
4. Latency-first evaluation. Add time-to-first-alert and time-to-stable-alert curves. Do not use point
adjustment. If a tolerant metric is retained, publish results at several buffers—e.g. 0, 1, 3 and 6
observation intervals—so tolerance cannot hide delayed alarms.
5. SSA-SHAD annotation schema. For each event, store onset/uncertainty interval, burn duration,
impulsive/finite-burn class, estimated Δv, affected residual channels, sensor/source transitions,
covariance quality and analyst confidence. Evaluate channel attribution with NDCG and maneuver
interpretation only when grounded in those structured fields.
6. Operational review-budget test. Simulate a ranked queue capped at k alerts per analyst shift.
Measure precision@k, missed-event cost, repeated-alert suppression and workload stability under
catalog growth. This would connect detector accuracy to an actual SSA operating concept.
