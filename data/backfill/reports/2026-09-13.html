1. MAD-LEO: A Maneuver-Annotated Orbital Dataset for LEO Satellites with Tiered Multi-Source Evidence

Zhixin Guo, Qi Shi, Xiaofan Xu, Linqiang Ge, Hua Zhu, Liyan Ben, Bendian Nie, Yuanrui Zhao, Xiaohan Li 8 September 2026 — arXiv preprint + public dataset

Direct paper — arXiv

This is the most important new item this week for your research.

The core contribution is not a detector but a real maneuver benchmark with independently sourced evidence. The mission-reported subset contains 1,134 maneuver events from 11 LEO geodetic/altimetry satellites, spanning 1992–2026. Labels come from mission-published maneuver histories rather than being generated by an anomaly detector. Each event is then cross-referenced with TLEs, precise-orbit products, and SLR observations. arXiv

The included spacecraft are TOPEX/Poseidon, Jason-1/2/3, CryoSat-2, HY-2A, SARAL/AltiKa, Sentinel-3A/3B, Sentinel-6A and SWOT. In addition, an operational subset contains operator ephemerides and TLEs for 6,785 Starlink spacecraft over 107 continuous hours. The Starlink subset is intentionally left unlabeled because no independently validated maneuver truth is available. arXiv

Dataset design

Each reported maneuver is surrounded by a 30 h evaluation window:

The asymmetry is deliberate because a maneuver may enter the public catalog only after some delay. Event windows are assigned an evidence tier:

There are 754 tier-A, 194 tier-B and 186 tier-C maneuver events. Importantly, these tiers characterize supporting evidence availability; they do not redefine the mission-reported label. arXiv

The release also contains 1,139 no-event windows, plus a 274-window subset matched for evidence coverage. Fourteen nominal windows are explicitly flagged as possible unreported maneuvers because their TLE-derived semi-major-axis response exceeds 20 m. arXiv

That last point is particularly important: the authors acknowledge that nominal labels are not necessarily perfect.

  • •

  • •
    43.36 million Starlink ephemeris-state rows;
  • •
    machine-readable provenance and quality flags. arXiv

The dataset is publicly released under CC BY 4.0. The authors also provide seven technical-validation experiment suites. arXiv

Detection latency

No ML detector is benchmarked as the main contribution, so there is no reported MTTD or event-delay curve. However, the dataset explicitly contains event epochs, operation intervals and a time_uncertainty_seconds field, which makes a proper latency evaluation possible. arXiv

This is a major improvement over benchmarks where maneuver truth is only an approximate timestamp.

Uncertainty

The paper does not provide probabilistic label uncertainty, but it exposes several forms of uncertainty explicitly:

  • •
    event-time uncertainty;
  • •
    missing evidence sources;
  • •
    source-specific coverage;
  • •
    source precision metadata where available;
  • •
    confidence tiers.

That is much better than collapsing every maneuver into an equally reliable binary label.

Main limitation

The 11 labeled satellites are not representative of the general LEO catalog. They are high-quality geodetic/altimetry missions for which precise OD and maneuver records happen to be available.

Computational cost

CPU-only retraining for Mission 1 averages roughly 1042 s per monthly window, with peak memory around 7.7 GB; Mission 2 requires about 215 s/window and 0.54 GB average peak memory. arXiv

So “deployment-ready” should be understood as ground-segment operational, not necessarily onboard.

Leakage concern

There is one methodological issue worth flagging.

Mission-level exploratory characterization is performed on the complete mission datasets, including future data, in order to justify choosing LSTM-AE for M1 and PCA/K-means/LOF for M2. The authors state that this analysis is descriptive and is not used for hyperparameter or threshold tuning. arXiv

Strictly speaking, however, model-family selection informed by whole-mission structure is still a mild form of future information usage. It is nowhere near test-label optimization, but a fully prospective deployment would need to select the detector using only accumulated historical data.

About 65% of CALM alarms in the real compressor experiment are rejected by PeriodicCALM as ordinary cyclic behavior. arXiv +1

Latency

The paper discusses computational latency, not event-onset delay.

Envelope construction/filtering remains below one second in the tested configurations, while fitting the underlying CALM detector dominates runtime. At least three complete cycles are recommended for training. arXiv

Uncertainty/calibration

Thresholding is bootstrap-based, with phase-specific empirical envelopes. This is not calibrated predictive uncertainty in the conformal sense and does not provide a guaranteed long-run FAR.

Main limitation

The method requires a known and reasonably stable cycle period .

Orbital data are more complicated because periodicity may exist at several frequencies:

  • •
    orbital period;
  • •
    nodal/precession timescales;
  • •
    sampling cadence;
  • •
    illumination/visibility cycles.

A single periodic envelope could therefore be too simplistic.
