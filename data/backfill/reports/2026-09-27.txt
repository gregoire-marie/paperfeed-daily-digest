Latency and uncertainty
This is fundamentally an uncertainty-structure paper. It does not evaluate anomaly-detection delay or
false-alarm rates.
Its important result is that marginal calibration is insufficient. A model may have well-calibrated intervals
for each orbital element while still producing the wrong joint covariance and therefore the wrong
Mahalanobis anomaly score.

The coupling assumes historical dependence remains relevant after the forecast origin. The separable
Gaussian construction cannot represent variable-specific lead–lag relationships, and the experiments
contain no orbital data or anomaly labels.

Data and evaluation

Important qualifications
The reported 4.30 ms is inference latency, not physical event-to-alarm delay.
RLS “uncertainty” is geometric novelty scaled by estimated residual noise; it is not a calibrated
posterior probability.
Long-term resistance to catastrophic forgetting is not demonstrated.
Feedback labels are assumed available from an operator.
Statistical fault injection may not represent physically valid faults.
arxiv.org
Relevance to your work
This suggests keeping your expensive TSMixer offline and distilling its score into a lightweight adaptive

fixed temporal representation + cheap adaptive readout.
For SSA, updates should be gated by catalog-quality checks or confirmed maneuvers, rather than
unrestricted self-training.

Pseudo-anomaly strategies
The paper tests:

feature-wise recombination;
sequence or element exchange;
examples from another domain;

a diverse union of several constructions.
This distinction matters: arbitrary Gaussian points are often too far from the nominal manifold to provide
useful model selection.
Evaluation
The study covers DCASE 2022–2025 anomalous-machine-sound benchmarks, four pretrained
embedding models and 208 detector configurations. It evaluates model-selection quality using Spearman
correlation, pairwise ranking accuracy and regret relative to the genuinely best detector.
arxiv.org +1
The diverse pseudo-AUC estimator obtains overall rank correlation 0.51, pairwise accuracy 0.69, and
regret 2.75%. It exceeds conventional development-set selection on the shifted evaluation sets by 1.63
percentage points in selected AUC, with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [0.85,2.45].
arxiv.org
Limitations
The evidence is exclusively from acoustic machine monitoring and nearest-neighbour detectors.
Performance depends strongly on pseudo-anomaly construction; sequence and element exchange
perform poorly, and the best construction uses semantic metadata that may not always be available.
There is no threshold calibration, latency analysis or false-alarm guarantee.
Relevance to your work
This is useful for selecting anomaly scores without tuning on test maneuvers. Physically meaningful SSA
pseudo-anomalies could be produced by applying small impulsive Δv perturbations to nominal
propagated trajectories:
Δv ∼ p(magnitude,direction,orbital phase).
The pseudo-events should be used only for model selection. Final performance must still be reported on
independent real maneuver labels such as MAD-LEO.

4. PISCES: Physics-Informed Solar-wind Convolutional autoEncoder for Space-
weather Anomaly Detection and Early Warning
Kevin Lee, Alison J. March
23 September 2026 — arXiv preprint; NASA symposium poster
Direct paper · Code
Method and data
PISCES is trained without event labels on one-minute OMNI solar-wind measurements. Its autoencoder
objective includes:
magnetic-field consistency;
an empirical temperature–velocity relationship;
Parker-spiral geometry;
penalties on changes in reconstructed derived quantities.
The final score is decomposed into 15 reconstruction, plasma, magnetic and physics-related terms rather
than producing only one opaque anomaly score.
arxiv.org
Training uses 11 years of nominally unlabeled data. Evaluation uses 2018–2024 events from NASA DONKI
and the Richardson–Cane interplanetary coronal mass ejection catalog. A 99th-percentile threshold is
fitted on 2016–2017 validation scores. Metrics include average precision, window and event probability of
detection, false-alarm ratio and detection offset.
arxiv.org
Results and timeliness
The selected checkpoint obtains test average precision 0.365, rising to 0.406 after validation-selected
skip attenuation. Across 11 runs, mean average precision rises from
0.306 ± 0.029
to
0.376 ± 0.020.
Using causal trailing-median smoothing, full-skip configurations match approximately 29–35% of sudden
commencements, with median estimated leads of 24–28 minutes. These are estimated physical warning
margins, not measured end-to-end alert latencies.
arxiv.org +1
Major evaluation problems
This paper is valuable partly because it exposes several failure modes:
The chosen checkpoint was selected from 27 configurations after inspecting corrected test metrics.
The skip-attenuation study was designed after test-set inspection.
An untrained network achieves average precision 0.337, higher than 10 of the 11 trained full-skip
runs.
Physics-loss ablations do not show a consistent average-precision improvement.
Changing the catalog matching tolerance from ±0.5 h to ±6 h raises PR-AUC from 0.220 to 0.419.
OMNI timestamps are shifted to predicted bow-shock arrival and do not represent measurement
receipt or alert issuance time.
arxiv.org +2
Therefore, the reported results are retrospective evidence, not a prospective demonstration of operational
early warning.
Relevance to your work
The transferable parts are:
1. physically decomposed anomaly scores;
2. explicit event-versus-window metrics;
3. sensitivity analysis for label-matching tolerance;
4. the warning that visually plausible “early detection” can be created by timestamp conventions.

5. Evaluating Accuracy and Probabilistic Reliability of Zero-Shot Time Series
Foundation Models
Panagiotis Michael, Moysis Symeonides, Demetris Trihinas
22 September 2026 — arXiv preprint
Direct paper · Benchmark code
Evaluation design
The study compares six zero-shot forecasting models:
Moirai-2;
TiRex;
Chronos-2;
Sundial;
Toto;
TimesFM;
against ARIMA, running average, random walk with drift and a supervised PatchTST.
A chronological non-overlapping rolling protocol varies both forecast horizon and context length. Data
include one-second traffic, hourly electricity load and daily financial prices. Two recent datasets were
chosen to reduce pretraining leakage risk.

Main result
Point accuracy and uncertainty reliability are not aligned.
On the long EdgeTraffic horizon:
TimesFM ICE: 0.73;
Moirai ICE: 0.56;
Toto ICE: 0.07;
Chronos-2 and TiRex: below 0.06.
TimesFM and Moirai remain competitive in point forecasting while becoming strongly overconfident. TiRex
is the most consistently conservative model; Chronos-2 gives the best overall accuracy–calibration
compromise.
arxiv.org +1
Limitations
The evaluation is univariate and covers only three application datasets. It does not test anomalies,
irregular sampling, missingness, concept drift, multivariate calibration or detection latency. Computational
cost is also deferred to future work.
arxiv.org
Relevance to your work
A forecasting model with lower mean squared error can still produce a worse detector if its predictive
intervals are too narrow:
underestimated uncertainty ⟹ inflated nominal residual scores ⟹ false alarms.
Your forecasting evaluation should therefore report coverage as a function of forecast horizon, spacecraft
and orbital regime—not just aggregate MSE.
