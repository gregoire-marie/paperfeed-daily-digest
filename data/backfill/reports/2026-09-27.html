Latency and uncertainty

This is fundamentally an uncertainty-structure paper. It does not evaluate anomaly-detection delay or false-alarm rates.

Its important result is that marginal calibration is insufficient. A model may have well-calibrated intervals for each orbital element while still producing the wrong joint covariance and therefore the wrong Mahalanobis anomaly score.

Limitations

The coupling assumes historical dependence remains relevant after the forecast origin. The separable Gaussian construction cannot represent variable-specific lead–lag relationships, and the experiments contain no orbital data or anomaly labels. arxiv.org

This distinction matters: arbitrary Gaussian points are often too far from the nominal manifold to provide useful model selection.

Evaluation

The study covers DCASE 2022–2025 anomalous-machine-sound benchmarks, four pretrained embedding models and 208 detector configurations. It evaluates model-selection quality using Spearman correlation, pairwise ranking accuracy and regret relative to the genuinely best detector. arxiv.org +1

Limitations

The evidence is exclusively from acoustic machine monitoring and nearest-neighbour detectors. Performance depends strongly on pseudo-anomaly construction; sequence and element exchange perform poorly, and the best construction uses semantic metadata that may not always be available.

There is no threshold calibration, latency analysis or false-alarm guarantee.

4. PISCES: Physics-Informed Solar-wind Convolutional autoEncoder for Space-weather Anomaly Detection and Early Warning

Kevin Lee, Alison J. March 23 September 2026 — arXiv preprint; NASA symposium poster Direct paper · Code

Method and data

PISCES is trained without event labels on one-minute OMNI solar-wind measurements. Its autoencoder objective includes:

  • •
    magnetic-field consistency;
  • •
    an empirical temperature–velocity relationship;
  • •
    Parker-spiral geometry;
  • •
    penalties on changes in reconstructed derived quantities.

The final score is decomposed into 15 reconstruction, plasma, magnetic and physics-related terms rather than producing only one opaque anomaly score. arxiv.org

Training uses 11 years of nominally unlabeled data. Evaluation uses 2018–2024 events from NASA DONKI and the Richardson–Cane interplanetary coronal mass ejection catalog. A 99th-percentile threshold is fitted on 2016–2017 validation scores. Metrics include average precision, window and event probability of detection, false-alarm ratio and detection offset. arxiv.org

Major evaluation problems

This paper is valuable partly because it exposes several failure modes:

  • •
    The chosen checkpoint was selected from 27 configurations after inspecting corrected test metrics.
  • •
    The skip-attenuation study was designed after test-set inspection.
  • •
    An untrained network achieves average precision , higher than 10 of the 11 trained full-skip runs.
  • •
    Physics-loss ablations do not show a consistent average-precision improvement.
  • •
    Changing the catalog matching tolerance from ±0.5 h to ±6 h raises PR-AUC from to .

    OMNI timestamps are shifted to predicted bow-shock arrival and do not represent measurement receipt or alert issuance time. arxiv.org +2

Therefore, the reported results are retrospective evidence, not a prospective demonstration of operational early warning.
