MAD-LEO: A Maneuver-Annotated Orbital Dataset for LEO Satellites with Tiered Multi-Source Evidence

Zhixin Guo, Qi Shi, Xiaofan Xu, Linqiang Ge +5 more · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

With the rapid development of aerospace technology and the large-scale deployment of low Earth orbit (LEO) constellations, the risk of orbital collisions has increased, creating a growing demand for reliable observations of satellite maneuvers. However, public datasets containing real maneuver records remain scarce. We present MAD-LEO, a Maneuver-Annotated orbital Dataset for LEO satellites. The mission-reported subset contains 1,134 maneuver events from eleven geodetic and altimetry satellites spanning 1992 to 2026, with labels taken directly from mission-published maneuver histories. Each event is checked against two-line element (TLE) data, precise orbit products, and satellite laser ranging (SLR) observations, with evidence tiers assigned according to data availability. The operational subset pairs operator-published ephemerides for 6,785 Starlink satellites with cataloged TLE records over a continuous 107-hour period. Technical validation across seven machine-readable experiment suites confirms the cross-source consistency of the labels and the evidence products.

Why it matters
Provides independent maneuver evidence needed to evaluate a detector without circular anomaly-derived ground truth.
Main idea
Assemble maneuver annotations with tiered evidence and explicit timing uncertainty.
Method
Cross-reference mission histories with TLEs, precise-orbit products, and SLR; distinguish mission-reported events from weaker candidate evidence.
Key results
The briefing reports 1,134 mission-reported maneuvers from 11 LEO satellites spanning 1992–2026.
Limitations
A dataset is not detector validation; label confidence, timing uncertainty, mission coverage, and evidence availability must inform evaluation.
Relevance to my research
Benchmark TSMixer on confirmed events with spacecraft-held-out splits and detection delay relative to the recorded timing uncertainty.

Structure-Aware Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Spacecraft Telemetry with Adaptive EVT Thresholding

Óscar Alcarria, Rafael Sánchez, Javier Sempere, Pablo Torrijos +4 more · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

Operational anomaly detection in spacecraft telemetry typically requires labeled historical anomalies or extended warm-up periods. These requirements are rarely met in practice. We propose an unsupervised, deployment-ready framework that produces predictions from the second month of operation without any labels, prior fault knowledge, or mission-specific tuning. The approach combines incremental monthly retraining, statistical model selection, and adaptive Extreme Value Theory (EVT) thresholding for false alarm control. On the ESA Anomalies Dataset (ESA-AD), it achieves $F_{0.5}=0.700$ on Mission~1 and $F_{0.5}=0.698$ on Mission~2 under strict chronological evaluation.

Why it matters
Shows a chronological, label-free spacecraft monitoring setup and a reusable adaptive-threshold component.
Main idea
Select lightweight telemetry detectors by mission structure and calibrate their score tails.
Method
Use an LSTM autoencoder or PCA/clustering/LOF, monthly causal retraining, and smoothed Generalized Pareto tail thresholds.
Key results
The briefing reports event precision/recall of 0.852/0.408 and 0.721/0.621 on two ESA missions.
Limitations
Tail exceedance targets are not guaranteed false-positive rates; six-hour alarm merging hides latency, and whole-mission model selection risks leakage.
Relevance to my research
Apply smoothed EVT thresholds to existing orbital residuals, comparing static calibration at matched event-level false alarms.

Online Change-Point Monitoring for Object-valued Time Series

Yi Zhang, Tim Kutta, Xiaofeng Shao · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

We develop closed- and open-end procedures for monitoring changes in the marginal distribution of object-valued time series. The method combines two distance-based Hilbert-space embeddings, a monitoring-time-dependent projection, and self-normalization. It is computable entirely from pairwise distances, does not require long-run variance estimation, and admits exact recursive updates. Under weak temporal dependence, the closed-end null limit is a pivotal Brownian functional. For monitoring over an unbounded horizon, we introduce a growing monitoring-time weight, establish a maximal inequality and uniform remote-tail control, and derive an open-end pivotal limit together with an equivalent fixed-interval representation for critical-value simulation. Both procedures are consistent against fixed marginal changes and retain the $n^{-1/2}$ and $n^{-1/4}$ local detection boundaries associated with the linear and quadratic projection signals. Simulations with distribution-valued time series illustrate favorable numerical properties, and an application to monthly stock-return distributions illustrates the usefulness of our procedure. Further simulations with graph-valued time series and an application to spatial point processes of seismic activity are reported in the supplementary material.

Why it matters
Explicitly handles serial dependence instead of applying IID change tests directly to correlated residual streams.
Main idea
Monitor changes in the distribution of vectors, graphs, or other object-valued observations.
Method
Use pairwise-distance Hilbert embeddings, time-dependent projection, self-normalization, and recursive updates under weak mixing assumptions.
Key results
The briefing describes open- and closed-end asymptotic calibration plus tests on distribution, graph, financial, and seismic data.
Limitations
Calibration is asymptotic rather than a finite-sample anytime guarantee; computation grows with monitoring history and no SSA test is reported.
Relevance to my research
Treat multivariate residual windows as objects and compare their structural changes with scalar residual-norm monitoring.

Real-time and adaptive anomaly detection algorithm for cyclostationary models

Justyna Witulska, Tomasz Barszcz, Ireneusz Jabłoński, Agnieszka Wyłomańska · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

This article introduces PeriodicCALM, an effective real-time anomaly detection framework designed for cyclostationary data streams. While classical cyclostationary processes feature periodically time-varying statistical properties, real-world signals often contain recurring impulsive components that conceal abnormal behavior. Existing real-time methods for struggle with these dynamics, frequently misinterpreting phase-dependent variability as non-cyclic anomalies and causing excessive false alarms. To address this, PeriodicCALM incorporates cycle-dependent variability to systematically ignore regular cyclic impulses while accurately isolating genuine anomalies. Operating in real time with continuous retraining capabilities, the method adapts dynamically to evolving signal characteristics. Comparative evaluations against the baseline CALM framework using simulated data demonstrate significant improvements in detection accuracy and training efficiency, alongside a reduction in prediction latency. Furthermore, the practical utility of PeriodicCALM is validated on real-world vibration signals collected from a compressor monitoring system.

Why it matters
Suppresses alarms caused by ordinary periodic behavior rather than assuming a global nominal distribution.
Main idea
Add phase-specific nominal envelopes to a baseline density-based detector.
Method
PeriodicCALM estimates per-phase quantile bounds across repeated cycles and rejects candidate alarms that remain inside their phase envelope.
Key results
The briefing reports rejection of about 65% of baseline alarms in compressor data, with large precision gains in cyclic simulations.
Limitations
Requires a known stable period; multiple orbital timescales and sampling changes complicate transfer, with no guaranteed operational false-alarm rate.
Relevance to my research
Check residual quantiles by orbital phase and compare phase-conditioned calibration with a global threshold.

Temporal and Multimodal Deep Learning for Cyberattack Detection in LEO Satellite Systems

Kyle Stein, Guillermo Francia III, Eman El-Sheikh, Hossain Shahriar · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

The growing reliance on Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite communication systems has increased the need for intelligent methods capable of detecting cyberattacks across complex and dynamic space environments. Unlike conventional network intrusion detection, satellite systems generate heterogeneous information across radio-frequency (RF) links, onboard hardware, and orbital operations. However, many existing approaches either rely on terrestrial intrusion datasets or evaluate individual observations independently, limiting their ability to capture temporal attack behavior specific to LEO satellites. In this work, we conduct a systematic study of deep-learning-based cyberattack detection using the recently introduced satellite-specific UNSW-IoTSAT dataset. We investigate structured learning architectures that preserve hardware, orbital, and RF information, including a Subsystem-Fusion MLP and a hierarchical multimodal Transformer that models both cross-subsystem interactions and temporal evolution. We further evaluate leakage-resistant row-level and temporal settings, along with cross-satellite generalization, to characterize how model architecture and evaluation protocol influence satellite cyberattack detection. Experimental results demonstrate the value of structured multimodal modeling and rigorous evaluation, with the hierarchical Transformer achieving up to 91.66% accuracy and 85.63% macro F1 under the leakage-resistant evaluation protocol.

Why it matters
Highlights satellite-specific temporal leakage and the need to measure transfer across spacecraft.
Main idea
Classify cyber anomalies using temporal and multimodal satellite subsystem information.
Method
Compare subsystem-fusion MLPs and hierarchical Transformers on UNSW-IoTSAT with leakage-resistant and cross-satellite evaluation.
Key results
The briefing reports macro-F1 up to 85.63% under the leakage-resistant protocol.
Limitations
Cyberattack classification differs from orbital maneuver detection; no calibrated alert rates or sequential event-delay evaluation is established.
Relevance to my research
Compare random-window, chronological, leave-one-spacecraft-out, and leave-one-regime-out splits to detect object-identity leakage.
