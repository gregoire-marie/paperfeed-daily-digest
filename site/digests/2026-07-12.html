1. Fast and Accurate Anomaly Detection in Time Series

Authors: Emanuele Mele, Massimo Cafaro, Angelo Coluccia, Italo Epicoco
Published: 2 July 2026
Type: Preprint
Priority: High—directly test as a statistical baseline

The paper introduces DWTt-test, an unsupervised, training-free detector combining a multilevel Haar Discrete Wavelet Transform with a specially derived Studentʼs -test applied over sliding windows. It produces pointwise anomaly scores from both coarse and detail coefficients, thereby covering slow trend deviations and localized high-frequency changes. arXiv +1

Evaluation

The authors evaluate on 343 univariate time-series datasets using:

  • •
    area under the receiver operating characteristic curve;
  • •
    area under the precision–recall curve;
  • •
    a time-aware area-under-curve metric;
  • •
    no point adjustment;
  • •
    threshold-independent comparisons.

This is a materially stronger evaluation protocol than the common practice of converting one correctly detected point into credit for an entire anomalous interval. The results reportedly outperform unsupervised baselines and recent self-supervised models, whose anomaly scores become substantially less competitive once point adjustment is removed. arXiv

Latency and computation

The method has claimed linear complexity and low CPU execution cost. The paper compares CPU execution on an Apple M1 Pro against deep models evaluated on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, explicitly acknowledging that these hardware results are not directly comparable. It evaluates processing latency per point, but does not evaluate event-level detection delay relative to anomaly onset. arXiv

Uncertainty

The -statistic provides a statistically interpretable score under the paperʼs assumed null distribution, but this is not equivalent to calibrated operational false-alarm probability when observations are autocorrelated, nonstationary, or affected by propagation-model bias. No empirical calibration analysis is presented for out-of-distribution series.

Implementation relevance

This is especially relevant as:

  • 1.
    a lightweight baseline against TSMixer residuals;
  • 2.
    a detector on individual equinoctial-element residual channels;
  • 3.
    a multiscale score generator that could be fused across channels;
  • 4.
    an ablation for determining whether the neural forecaster adds information beyond multiresolution change detection.
Limitations and risks
  • •
    Univariate only; direct multivariate support is future work.
  • •
    Requires padding to a power-of-two series length.
  • •
    Assumes anomalies are sufficiently distinct from the nominal distribution.
  • •
    The code is not yet public; the authors state it will be released upon acceptance.
  • •
    No satellite data, orbit-specific perturbations, or event-level latency evaluation.
  • •
    Hyperparameter tuning across 343 datasets should be checked carefully once code is available. arXiv +1

Recommended experiment: apply DWTt-test independently to normalized forecast residuals for , then combine channel -values or ranks using Fisher, Cauchy, and maximum-statistic fusion. Evaluate both pointwise metrics and maneuver-level time-to-detection.

2. A Self-Supervised Framework for Space Object Behaviour Characterisation

Authors: Ian Groves, Andrew Campbell, James Fernandes, Diego Ramírez Rodríguez, Paul Murray, Massimiliano Vasile, Victoria Nockles
Latest revision: 27 April 2026
Type: Revised preprint
Priority: High—most directly relevant space-domain representation work

The framework uses a Perceiver Variational Autoencoder trained through self-supervised reconstruction and masked reconstruction on 227,000 real light curves from the MMT-9 observatory. The learned representation is subsequently used for reconstruction-based anomaly detection, motion-mode prediction, and synthetic light-curve generation. Fine-tuning data come from the CASSANDRA and GRIAL simulators using several spacecraft geometries, including Sentinel-3, Soil Moisture and Ocean Salinity, Starlink, and generic box-wing platforms. arXiv

Evaluation

Reported results include:

  • •
    reconstruction mean squared error: 0.009;
  • •
    anomaly-detection accuracy: 85%;
  • •
    anomaly-detection receiver operating characteristic area under curve: 0.92;
  • •
    motion-mode accuracy: 82%;
  • •
    motion-mode receiver operating characteristic area under curve: 0.95.

The paper also examines high-confidence predictions on real light curves and reports recognizable effects such as glints and object-specific profiles. arXiv

Latency

No meaningful online detection-latency evaluation is reported. Light curves are handled as observation sequences or windows, so deployment delay would depend on:

  • •
    required light-curve duration;
  • •
    observation cadence;
  • •
    masking/window design;
  • •
    threshold persistence;
  • •
    observatory revisit rate.

Thus the reported classification quality cannot be interpreted as rapid maneuver detection.

Uncertainty

The Variational Autoencoder provides a probabilistic latent model, but the published headline metrics do not establish:

  • •
    calibration of anomaly probabilities;
  • •
    epistemic uncertainty under unseen spacecraft geometries;
  • •
    false-alarm guarantees;
  • •
    confidence under simulator-to-real domain shift.

Reconstruction difficulty is used as an anomaly indicator, but low likelihood or high reconstruction error does not uniquely imply anomalous physical behaviour.

Implementation relevance

Although the observation modality differs from orbital-element residuals, the architecture suggests several transferable ideas:

  • •
    heterogeneous or irregularly sampled inputs through Perceiver-style cross-attention;
  • •
    large-scale self-supervised pretraining before maneuver-specific fine-tuning;
  • •
    masked reconstruction as a pretext task;
  • •
    joint learning of reconstruction, forecasting, and behavioural state;
  • •
    synthetic-to-real training with separate simulators.
Limitations and leakage risks
  • •
    The downstream anomaly labels are primarily simulator-derived.
  • •
    Reported accuracy may partly reflect simulator, spacecraft-geometry, or rendering signatures rather than genuine behavioural differences.
  • •
    Random splitting of windows from the same spacecraft or simulated trajectory could cause substantial leakage; splits should be verified at object, geometry, simulator, and time-period levels.
  • •
    No event-level false-alarm rate, mean time between false alarms, or detection delay.
  • •
    Real-data analysis is exploratory rather than a labeled operational validation. arXiv

Recommended experiment: reproduce the pretraining concept on unlabeled orbital histories, but force evaluation splits by NORAD object and orbital regime. Probe whether the latent space preserves maneuver time, , burn direction, and duration—not merely satellite identity or altitude regime.

3. Aionoscope: Debugging Latent-State Accessibility in Time-Series Representations

Authors: Alexander Chemeris, Ming Jin, Randall Balestriero
Published: 1 July 2026
Type: Workshop preprint and open diagnostic benchmark
Priority: High—important evaluation methodology

Aionoscope is not an anomaly detector. It is a diagnostic framework that tests whether a frozen time-series representation preserves latent process variables that matter operationally, including:

  • •
    event timing;
  • •
    phase;
  • •
    amplitude;
  • •
    frequency;
  • •
    regime;
  • •
    component presence.

It generates seeded synthetic streams with exact categorical and continuous latent labels, then evaluates frozen encoders through standardized pooled linear probes. The first release compares 37 model– adapter combinations. arXiv

Main result

Many representations make coarse signal-component presence easy to recover but fail to expose continuous state accurately. The best reported dense-state score is approximately 0.689, compared with 0.999 for an oracle using the true dense features. The authors explicitly frame the current release as a diagnostic unit test rather than a stable leaderboard or evidence of real-task transfer. arXiv

Why this matters for maneuver detection

A forecasting model can achieve low mean squared error while suppressing exactly the rare variables needed for detection. For maneuver analysis, a useful representation should retain:

  • •
    onset epoch;
  • •
    maneuver duration;
  • •
    residual growth rate;
  • •
    affected orbital-element subspace;
  • •
    approximate impulse direction;
  • •
    uncertainty or regime state.

A model that only distinguishes “nominal versus unusual-looking window” may be inadequate for detection latency, maneuver characterization, or downstream orbit determination.

Evaluation quality

Strengths:

  • •
    deterministic generation;
  • •
    exact dense ground truth;
  • •
    common probe protocol;
  • •
    source artifacts and benchmark repository;
  • •
    explicit separation of representation quality from task-head capacity.

Weaknesses:

  • •
    only one training stream and probe-seed schedule in the initial snapshot;
  • •
    synthetic primitive mixtures;
  • •
    linear accessibility does not measure nonlinear recoverability;
  • •
    no anomaly-detection or orbit-domain transfer result;
  • •
    pooled representations may hide localized information that remains available at token level. arXiv +1
Latency and uncertainty

The benchmark does not measure detection delay or uncertainty. Its contribution is upstream: it can determine whether the representation retains maneuver onset and state variables before a detector is designed.

Recommended experiment: construct an orbital Aionoscope using controlled propagations with injected impulsive and finite-duration maneuvers. Probe frozen TSMixer hidden states for:

along with drag coefficient, orbital regime, observation cadence, and noise level. This would test whether forecasting representations preserve detection-relevant physics.

4. Self-Adaptive Anomaly Detection with Reinforcement Learning and Human Feedback in Connected Vehicles

Authors: Matthias Weiß, Athreya Hosahalli Prakash, Maurice Artelt, Falk Dettinger, Nasser Jazdi, Michael Weyrich
Published: 9 July 2026
Type: Preprint
Priority: Medium-high—deployment architecture rather than detector innovation

The paper proposes an online supervisory system combining:

  • 1.
    a factorized Deep Q-Network that selects one anomaly detector per monitored service;
  • 2.
    an attention mechanism for inter-service dependencies;
  • 3.
    three statistical concept-drift detectors;
  • 4.
    a unanimity rule for triggering adaptation;
  • 5.
    expert validation;
  • 6.
    replay-based retraining with 60% recent and 40% historical samples. arXiv
Evaluation

The system is evaluated on seven backend microservices in an automated-valet-parking testbed. The attention-based selection agent achieves:

  • •
    F1: 0.6915;
  • •
    recall: 0.6755;
  • •
    precision: 0.7076.

Individual detectors applied uniformly across services reach at most approximately 0.11 F1. After a real software update, performance on the new distribution falls to 0.52 and recovers to roughly 0.65 after retraining, while old-distribution F1 remains near 0.69. arXiv +1

Detection latency

The software update occurs at time step 2000, while the three-detector ensemble reaches unanimous drift agreement at step 2600. With one-second sampling, adaptation is triggered after approximately 600 seconds. The paper clearly identifies this as a precision–latency tradeoff caused by requiring all detectors to agree. arXiv

This is one of the few recent papers to report a concrete operational delay, although it concerns concept-drift detection, not anomaly-event detection.

Uncertainty and false-alarm control

There is no calibrated predictive uncertainty. False-alarm control is architectural: unanimous agreement among Page–Hinkley, Kolmogorov–Smirnov, and Mahalanobis-based detectors. This may increase precision but has no formal guarantee and can miss drift types invisible to any one required detector.

Relevance to SSA

A satellite-specific analogue could select among:

  • •
    forecasting-residual detectors;
  • •
    orbit-determination innovation tests;
  • •
    wavelet detectors;
  • •
    change-point methods;
  • •
    object- or regime-specific models.

The candidate detector could depend on altitude, eccentricity, drag conditions, data cadence, satellite class, and recent residual statistics.

Limitations
  • •
    Only one principal real update event is used for the drift demonstration.
  • •
    The dramatic improvement over individual detectors partly reflects an unfairly restrictive baseline: each individual detector is applied uniformly to heterogeneous streams, while the agent selects per stream.
  • •
    Expert labels are simulated from injected errors.
  • •
    No comparison against oracle model selection, static per-service selection, contextual bandits, or supervised gating.
  • •
    No uncertainty calibration or cost-sensitive evaluation.
  • •
    F1 averaging details and episode-level split independence require scrutiny. arXiv

Recommended experiment: before adopting reinforcement learning, compare a contextual selector against simpler alternatives: validation-optimal detector per orbital regime, gradient-boosted gating, Bayesian model averaging, and online Hedge/Exp4 weighting. Reinforcement learning is justified only if detector choice has delayed consequences or adaptation costs.

5. AstroMind: A High-Fidelity Benchmark for Spacecraft Behavior Reasoning Based on Large Language Models

Authors: Hao Liu, Siyuan Yang, Qinglei Hu, Dongyu Li
Published: 23 May 2026
Type: Preprint and benchmark
Priority: Medium—downstream interpretation, not primary detection

AstroMind evaluates spacecraft-behavior reasoning across:

  • •
    intent inference;
  • •
    maneuver-parameter estimation;
  • •
    threat assessment.

Scenarios are generated from high-fidelity astrodynamics simulations with observation noise, physical constraints, and textual intelligence sources assigned different reliability levels. Evaluation combines semantic correctness with numerical and physics-consistency measures. arXiv

Results

No model dominates all tasks:

  • •
    Qwen3 32B leads intent inference;
  • •
    QwQ 32B leads threat assessment and gives the lowest median relative error on parsed numerical items;
  • •
    GPT-OSS 20B receives the strongest judged reasoning scores and extracts the most scalar values;
  • •
    structured reasoning prompts improve the evaluated 8B models. arXiv +1
Relevance

The useful contribution is the separation between:

This prevents a common category error: high detection accuracy does not imply reliable intent inference.

For your pipeline, AstroMind could serve as a downstream evaluation framework after an independent time-series detector estimates maneuver evidence. The Large Language Model should consume structured outputs—estimated onset, orbital-element residuals, uncertainty, conjunction context—not raw time-series data alone.

Latency and uncertainty

The paper does not report online detection latency because detection is not its task. It introduces noisy sensing and intelligence with varying reliability, but this is not the same as calibrated output uncertainty. Large Language Model confidence should not be treated as posterior probability.

Limitations and leakage risks
  • •
    Predominantly synthetic scenarios.
  • •
    Benchmark contamination is possible once scenarios are public.
  • •
    Judge-model evaluation may introduce model-family and phrasing bias.
  • •
    Parameter extraction success varies substantially, so aggregate scores may conflate failure to emit a value with numerical estimation error.
  • •
    Intent labels derive from scenario construction and may be less ambiguous than real adversarial behavior.
  • •
    No end-to-end assessment from raw observations through detection to intent classification. arXiv

Recommended experiment: keep the detector and reasoning system separate. Generate a structured maneuver evidence object containing point estimates, covariance, alternative hypotheses, and provenance, then evaluate reasoning under controlled perturbations of each field.

Probe each frozen hidden layer for:
