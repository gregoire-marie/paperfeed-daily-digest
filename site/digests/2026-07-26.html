Exploring Zero-Shot Foundation Models for Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection

Martin Uray, Saverio Messineo, Roland Kwitt, Stefan Huber · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

Multivariate Time Series Anomaly Detection (MTSAD) is essential for reliability and safety in domains such as industrial process monitoring and financial risk management, yet conventional approaches rely on application-specific models that are costly to train and hard to scale. Foundation Models (FMs), pre-trained on broad data with strong zero-shot generalization, have recently become available for univariate time series forecasting, raising the question of whether they can address MTSAD without task-specific training. We investigate the zero-shot application of a univariate forecasting FM, TimesFM, to industrial MTSAD on the Secure Water Treatment (SWaT) benchmark, evaluating two strategies: treating the FM as a per-feature forecaster with thresholded prediction errors, and as an embedder whose intermediate representations feed standard outlier detectors. Neither of our proposed setups is competitive with established baselines; embeddings reveal only partial separation between normal and anomalous segments, insufficient for reliable detection. The cause is that the FM is too effective at capturing temporal dynamics, yielding low error even within fully anomalous windows, so persistent anomalies become indistinguishable from normal behavior. However, these observations yield valuable insights: the error peaks at anomaly boundaries, indicating FMs reliably detect distribution changes. We conclude that the proposed naive zero-shot FMs are unsuitable for MTSAD but promising for change-point detection.

Why it matters
Exposes a failure mode where better forecasting can erase the anomaly signal after a new regime becomes predictable.
Main idea
Evaluate zero-shot TimesFM forecasts and representations for multivariate anomaly detection.
Method
Forecast each channel independently and threshold residuals; also inspect latent separation on the SWaT benchmark.
Key results
The briefing reports weak anomaly discrimination and errors concentrated at regime boundaries, rather than throughout persistent anomalies.
Limitations
One industrial benchmark and a particular zero-shot setup do not establish limitations of fine-tuned or probabilistic foundation models.
Relevance to my research
Compare adaptive forecasts with a frozen nominal reference to test whether maneuver evidence disappears after onset.

Dual-Score Association-Discrepancy Enhancement for Early-Warning Multivariate Time-Series Anomaly Detection

Zhipeng Xiang, Limin Yang, Wen Chen · Preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

Multivariate time-series anomaly detection is important for server monitoring, industrial systems, and intelligent operation and maintenance. Existing point-wise and point-adjusted metrics often fail to show whether an anomalous segment is detected early enough for intervention. This paper proposes a lightweight dual-score enhancement for Anomaly Transformer. The method retains the original multiplicative anomaly score and adds window-normalized reconstruction error and association discrepancy terms, so weak devi- ations near anomaly onsets become more visible without changing the backbone network or using additional labels. Experiments are conducted on four public multivariate bench- marks, namelySMD,PSM,SMAP,andMSL,underthreerandomseeds. Comparedwiththe original Anomaly Transformer score, the proposed early-warning union mode improves Raw Event Recall by 0.3435, 0.2408, 0.0995, and 0.0278 on SMD, PSM, SMAP, and MSL, respectively, and reduces mean detection delay by 53.66%, 49.97%, 52.69%, and 39.74%. Additional range- and proximity-aware metrics further show that the score-level enhance- ment improves segment coverage and early-warning behavior, while AUPRC remains dataset-dependent. The results indicate that association-discrepancy score enhancement is a practical complement to existing Transformer-based anomaly detectors when early warning is more important than point-wise matching alone.

Why it matters
Focuses on early event detection rather than only improving pointwise or point-adjusted classification scores.
Main idea
Combine complementary scores from an existing Anomaly Transformer without changing its backbone.
Method
Mix the original multiplicative score with an association-discrepancy score and compare combined decisions across three random seeds.
Key results
The briefing reports increased raw event recall and reduced mean detection delay, evaluated with event, range, and proximity metrics.
Limitations
Extra alarms may explain some delay gains; threshold selection and nonstandard metric implementations require scrutiny.
Relevance to my research
Combine residual magnitude with inter-element dependency changes, evaluating delay at matched false alarms rather than separately optimized F1.

ClouDens: Operational Context-Aware Anomaly Detection for Large-scale Cloud System Monitoring

Thu T. H. Doan, Mohammad Saiful Islam, Andriy Miranskyy, Ngoc-Thanh Nguyen +2 more · arXiv preprint · 2026

Source PDF

Abstract

With the rapid growth of cloud computing infrastructures in scale and complexity, network monitoring for Large-scale Cloud Systems (LCSs) has become increasingly challenging, requiring automated and reliable anomaly detection to maintain service availability. Modern LCSs continuously generate telemetry logs from distributed cloud services, producing high-dimensional multivariate time series that capture system operations. Detecting anomalies in this context is difficult due to extreme dimensionality, complex dependencies among distributed components, and severe sparsity from intermittently active services. Taking these challenges into account, we first conduct an empirical study on telemetry logs from the IBM Cloud Console platform, and then propose ClouDens, an anomaly detection framework tailored to LCS monitoring that leverages operational-context attributes encoded in the telemetry log schema to improve detection accuracy and early identification of anomalies. ClouDens partitions high-dimensional telemetry logs into domain-guided subsets, constructs a context-aware graph modeling operational service dependencies, and employs Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks for forecasting-based anomaly detection. We evaluate ClouDens on the recently released IBM Cloud Telemetry Dataset and provide practical insights into designing reliable anomaly detection solutions for LCS monitoring. Results show ClouDens achieves higher NAB scores in count-based telemetry features, indicating more accurate, earlier anomaly detection with broader coverage than a GRU-based model. Our study further reveals that telemetry feature subsets, operational-context modeling, scoring strategies, and sparsity imputation all substantially influence detection performance, offering practical guidance for designing and fairly benchmarking anomaly detection approaches for LCS monitoring.

Why it matters
Uses known system structure to organize sparse telemetry instead of expecting one model to learn all dependencies.
Main idea
Partition variables into contextual groups and combine their forecasting-based anomaly decisions.
Method
Context-informed graphs feed group-specific spatiotemporal GNN forecasters; residual scores are aggregated through an ensemble.
Key results
The briefing reports improved NAB scores, coverage, and early detection on real IBM cloud telemetry with incident-derived labels.
Limitations
Cloud incidents differ from orbital maneuvers; a narrow GRU baseline and ensemble alert volume limit operational conclusions.
Relevance to my research
Group orbital features by physical sensitivity or sensor provenance and compare context-informed dependencies with unconstrained attention.

Mamba-AD: Linear-Complexity State Space Model with Selective Gating Mechanism for Long-Term Time Series Anomaly Detection

Unknown authors · Conference chapter · 2026

Source

Abstract

Accurate anomaly detection in high-dimensional, long-term industrial time series is essential for ensuring system reliability. Although Transformer-based models have achieved state-of-the-art performance, they suffer from quadratic computational complexity and a lack of global periodic awareness. Recently, Structured State Space Models (SSMs), particularly Mamba, have emerged as efficient alternatives; however, standard Mamba architectures face three critical challenges: global “common sense” deficiency, hidden state contamination by outliers, and causal limitations in sequence scanning. In this paper, we propose Mamba-AD, a linear-complexity framework designed to address these bottlenecks. Specifically, we first introduce a Multi-Scale Frequency Injection (MSFI) module that acts as a “common sense brain” by embedding global periodic priors into the model via frequency-domain analysis. Second, we develop a Selective Gating Mechanism (SGM), which functions as a dynamic firewall to prevent sudden anomalies from “polluting” the long-term hidden memory. Third, a Bidirectional State Fusion (BSF) mechanism is integrated to capture comprehensive contextual dependencies from both past and future perspectives. Extensive experiments on five benchmark datasets, including MSL and WADI, demonstrate that Mamba-AD significantly outperforms existing Transformer and Diffusion-based counterparts in F1-score. Furthermore, due to its linear scaling, Mamba-AD reduces inference latency by up to 45% on high-dimensional data, providing a highly efficient and robust solution for real-time industrial anomaly monitoring.

Method

Interpretable DualCross-Attention for Time Series Anomaly Detection

Unknown authors · Conference chapter · 2026

Source

Abstract

In recent years, research on multivariate time series anomaly detection has increasingly focused on leveraging Transformer architectures to address challenges in modeling long-range dependencies and complex inter-variable interactions. However, traditional Transformer frameworks operate within a unified representation space, making it difficult to simultaneously capture temporal dynamics and inter-variable relationships, thereby limiting their representation capacity. To address this issue, this paper constructs a dual-branch Transformer model that generates temporal-dominant and variable-dominant feature representations in parallel. A key challenge then lies in effectively fusing these two types of homologous yet heterogeneous representations. Single-path fusion methods tend to suffer from insufficient interaction and static integration, while multi-path fusion approaches often lead to coarse-grained interactions and the so-called “island effect.” To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a dual cross-attention mechanism to enable deep fusion of homologous heterogeneous data, and further introduces symmetric KL divergence to mitigate distribution inconsistencies that may arise during the fusion process. Moreover, to address the lack of transparency in decision-making when integrating heterogeneous features, this paper proposes a differential attention visualization method to reveal the key factors underlying model decisions. Experimental results on five benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance in anomaly detection, while its interpretability is validated through representative case studies.
